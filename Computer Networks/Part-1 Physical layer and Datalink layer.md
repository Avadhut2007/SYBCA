## ANALOG DATA AND DIGITAL DATA

Data (in Networking)
Data is the collection of raw facts, figures, or information that is intended to be communicated, stored, or processed by devices in a network.

It can be text, audio, video, images, or binary information.
Data is what we want to transmit from one device to another.
Data cannot be transmitted directly over a medium without being converted into a signal.

For Example:
Digital data to Digital signal: For short-distance transmission (like inside a computer or over Ethernet cables).

Digital data to Analog signal: For long-distance transmission (like over telephone lines or radio waves), using modulation.

## ANALOG SIGNAL

An analog signal is a type of signal that represents continuous data using a continuous range of values, meaning it can take any value within a given range. It is smooth and continuously varying in nature.

For example, a sound wave in analog form is represented by a continuously changing electrical signal that follows the variations in air pressure caused by the sound.

## DIGITAL SIGNAL

A digital signal is a type of signal that represents data as a sequence of discrete values, usually in binary form (0 and 1), using specific voltage levels. Unlike analog signals, digital signals do not vary continuously; they are discrete and quantized, meaning they take only distinct values.

Widely used in communication and computing systems for transmitting, processing, and storing information, including applications in telecommunications, audio and video systems, and computer networks.

## BIT RATE

Term-bit rate (instead of frequency) is used to describe digital signals.

The bit rate is the number of bits sent in one second, expressed in bits per second (bps).
Ex.
Assume we need to download text documents at the rate of 100 pages per minute. What is the required bit rate of the channel?

Solution
A page is an average of 24 lines with 80 characters in each line. If we assume that one character requires 8 bits, the bit rate is

$$
100 \times 24 \times 80 \times 8=1536000 \mathrm{bits} / \mathrm{min}=25600 \mathrm{bps}
$$

## DIGITAL-TO-DIGITAL TRANSMISSION

In this section, we see how we can represent digital data by using digital signals. The conversion involves three techniques: line coding, block coding, and scrambling. Line coding is always needed; block coding and scrambling may or may not be needed.

## LINE CODING

Converting a string of 1’s and 0’s (digital data) into a sequence of signals that denote the 1’s and 0’s.

For example a high voltage level (+V) could represent a “1” and a low voltage level (0 or -V) could represent a “0”.

## No DC component -

- It is the Average Voltage of the Transmitted Signal.
- Ideally, the average should be zero volts, meaning no DC (direct current) content.
- Why it Matters:
- 0 A strong DC component cannot pass through transformers or AC-coupled circuits.
- It also causes baseline wander, making it harder for the receiver to distinguish between 1s and Os.

![](Part-1%20Physical%20layer%20and%20Datalink%20layer/images649e670f-8a43-4d3d-bf71-487c1a776b60-18_705_2012_1127_414.jpg)

## CHARACTERISTICS

## Baseline wandering Prevention -

The average signal power at the receiver is considered the “baseline.” The value of a data element i.e. ‘1’ or ‘0’ at the receiver is determined against this baseline reference.

If the incoming signal does not vary over a long period of time, the baseline will drift called baseline wandering and thus cause errors in detection of incoming data elements.

## CHARACTERISTICS

Self-synchronization -

- The clocks at the sender and the receiver must have the same bit interval.

If the receiver’s bit intervals correspond exactly to the Sender’s bit Intervals, only then is it possible to receive a Signal correctly.

The clock frequencies of $T x$ and $R x$ should be the same.
If the clock frequencies of $T x$ and $R x$ are different, then data at $T x$ and $R x$ will be different.
So to match data at $T x$ and $R x$, clock frequencies should be the same and synchronized.

- A long sequence of 0’s or 1’s may cause the receiver to go out of sync. and identify the data bit wrongly.
- This can be achieved if there are transitions in the signal that alert the receiver to the beginning, middle, or end of the pulse.
- If the receiver’s clock is out of synchronization, these points can reset the clock.

## CHARACTERISTICS

Built in Error detection -

- Errors occur during transmission due to line impairments.
- Some codes are constructed such that when an error occurs it can be detected.

## POLAR - NRZ

The voltages are on both sides of the time axis.
Polar NRZ scheme can be implemented with two voltages. E.g. +V for 1 and -V for 0.

There are two versions:

- NZR - Level (NRZ-L) - positive voltage for one symbol and negative for the other symbol.
- NRZ - Inversion (NRZ-I) - the change or lack of change in polarity determines the value of a symbol. E.g. a “1” symbol inverts the polarity , a “0” does not.

## POLAR - NRZ

In NRZ-L the value of the bit determines the level of the voltage. In NRZ-I the value of the next bit determines the inversion or the lack of inversion.

NRZ-L and NRZ-I both have a DC component problem and baseline wandering; it is worse for NRZ-L. Both have no self synchronization & no error detection.

But Both are relatively simple to implement.

Polar NRZ-L and NRZ-I schemes

## POLAR - RZ

The Return to Zero (RZ) scheme uses three voltage values. +, 0 , -.

Each symbol has a transition in the middle of its bit period. Either from high to zero or from low to zero.

Polar RZ scheme

![](Part-1%20Physical%20layer%20and%20Datalink%20layer/images649e670f-8a43-4d3d-bf71-487c1a776b60-32_825_2393_302_65.jpg)

Advantages:-
No DC components or baseline wandering.
Self synchronization - transition indicates symbol value.
Disadvantages:-
More complex as it uses three voltage level. It has no error detection capability.

This scheme has more signal transitions (two per symbol) and therefore requires a wider bandwidth.

## POLAR - BIPHASE: MANCHESTER AND DIFFERENTIAL MANCHESTER

Manchester coding consists of combining the NRZ-L and RZ schemes.

- Every symbol has a level transition in the middle: from high to low or low to high. Uses only two voltage levels.

Differential Manchester coding consists of combining the NRZ-I and RZ schemes.

- Every symbol has a level transition in the middle. But the level at the beginning of the symbol is determined by the symbol value. One symbol causes a level change the other does not.

Polar biphase: Manchester and differential Manchester schemes

![](Part-1%20Physical%20layer%20and%20Datalink%20layer/images649e670f-8a43-4d3d-bf71-487c1a776b60-35_1463_2409_308_34.jpg)

In Manchester and differential Manchester encoding, the transition at the middle of the bit is used for synchronization.

The bandwidth of Manchester and differential Manchester is 2 times that of NRZ. There is no DC component and no baseline wandering.

None of these codes has error detection.

## BIPOLAR - AMI AND PSEUDOTERNARY

Code uses 3 voltage levels: +, 0, -, to represent the symbols.

Voltage level for one symbol is at “0” and the other alternates between + & -.

Bipolar Alternate Mark Inversion (AMI) - the “0” symbol is represented by zero voltage and the “1” symbol alternates between +V and -V.

Pseudoternary is the reverse of AMI.

Bipolar schemes: AMI and Pseudoternary

## SERIAL TRANSMISSION

Serial transmission is a method of data communication where bits are sent sequentially, one at a time, over a single wire or communication channel.

It is commonly used for long-distance communication and in modern, high-speed computer interfaces (like USB or SATA) because it requires fewer cables, is more cost-effective, and reduces signal interference compared to parallel transmission.

Sender and receiver must contain a hardware that converts data from the parallel form used in the device to the serial form used on the wire

## TRANSMISSION ORDER: BITS AND BYTES

In serial mode, when sending bits, which bit should be sent across the medium first?

Should a sender transmit

- the Most Significant Bit (MSB)
- or the Least Significant Bit (LSB) first?

little-endian describes a system that sends the LSB first.
big-endian describes a system that sends the MSB first.
Either form can be used, but the sender and receiver must agree.

## SERIAL TRANSMISSION

Three broad categories:

- Synchronous transmission occurs continuously with no gap between the transmission of two data items.
- Asynchronous transmission can occur at any time with an arbitrary delay between the transmission of two data items.
- Isochronous transmission occurs at regular intervals with a fixed gap between the transmission of two data items.

## SYNCHRONOUS TRANSMISSION

A synchronous mechanism transmits bits of data continually

- with no gap between bits
- after transmitting the final bit of one data byte, the sender transmits a bit of the next data byte

There is no delimiter to identify the start and end of the frame or byte.

The receiver receives the bits and then groups them appropriately.

The sender and receiver constantly remain synchronized.
Need to use self-synchronizing codes.

## ASYNCHRONOUS TRANSMISSION

In asynchronous allows the physical medium to be idle for an arbitrary time between two transmissions

The asynchronous style of communication is well-suited to applications that generate data randomly.

It is typically used for request -reply kind of application.
Asynchronous technologies arrange few extra bits before each data item is transmitted to inform the receiver that a data transfer is starting

These extra bits allow the receiver to synchronize with the incoming signal
Extra bits are known as a preamble or start bits

## DIGITAL-TO-ANALOG CONVERSION

The process of changing one of the characteristics of an analog signal based on the information in digital data.

ASK (Amplitude Shift Keying): Amplitude changes between high and low to represent 1 or 0.

FSK (Frequency Shift Keying): Frequency changes to represent 1 or 0.
PSK (Phase Shift Keying): Phase changes to represent 1 or 0.
QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation): Combines ASK and PSK for higher data rates.

## T

- A hub is used as a central point of connection among media segments.
- A Hub is a hardware device used to connect several computers together.
- A hub contains multiple ports. Cables from network devices plug in to the ports on the hub.

Types of HUBS :-

- A passive hub is just a connector. It connects the wires coming from different branches.
    - The signal pass through a passive hub without regeneration or amplification.
    - Connect several networking cables together
- Active hubs or Multiport repeaters- They regenerate or amplify the signal before they are retransmitted.

## T

- Hubs Acts on the physical layer Operate on bits rather than frames also called multiport repeater
- Used to connect stations adapters in a physical star topology but logically bus Connection to the hub consists of two pairs of twisted pair wire one for transmission and the other for receiving.
- Hub receives a bit from an adapter and sends it to all the other adapters without implementing any access method. does not do filtering (forward a frame into a specific destination or drop it) just it copy the received frame onto all other links.

## T

- The entire hub forms a single collision domain, and a single Broadcast domain
    - Collision domain: is that part of the network (set of NICs) when two or more nodes transmit at the same time collision will happen.
    - Broadcast domain: is that part of the network (set of NIC) where each NIC can ‘see’ other NICs’ traffic broadcast messages.
- Multiple Hubs can be used to extend the network length
- For 10BaseT and 100BaseT the maximum length of the connection between an adapter and the hub is 100 meters.

## HUB

Hub Advantages:

- Simple, inexpensive device
- Multi-tier provides graceful degradation
- Portions of the LAN continue to operate if one hub malfunctions
- As an active hubs regenerate signals, it increases the distance that can be spanned by the LAN (up to 100 meters per segment).
- Hubs can also be connected locally to a maximum of two other hubs, thereby increasing the number of devices that can be attached to the LAN.
- Active hubs are usually used against attenuation, which is a decrease in the strength of the signal over distance.

## HUB

- Hub Disadvantages:
    - Bandwidth is shared by all hosts i.e. 10Mbs shared by 25 ports/users.
    - Can create bottlenecks when used with switches.
    - Have no layer 3 switching capability.
    - Most Hubs are unable to utilize VLANS
    - Individual segment collision domains become one large collision domain (reduce the performance) can’t interconnect different Ethernet technologies (like 10BaseT & 100BaseT) because no buffering at the hub

- Can have two or more interfaces

## PHYSICAL LAYER DEVICES

Hubs:
Passive Hubs:
A passive hub is just a connector. It connects the wires coming from different branches. In a star-topology Ethernet LAN, a passive hub is just a point where signals from different stations collide.
Active Hubs:
An active hub is actually a multipart repeater. It is typically used to connect stations in a physical star topology. However, hubs can also be used to create multiple hierarchical levels. The hierarchical use of hubs removes the 10Base-T (100 m) length limit.

## PHYSICAL LAYER DEVICES

Repeaters:
Repeaters are used in transmission systems to regenerate analog or digital signals distorted by transmission loss. Analog repeaters can only amplify the signal, whereas digital repeaters can reproduce a signal to near its original quality.
